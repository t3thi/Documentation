# Machine-assisted Core inventories require a human evidence gate

## Statement

Machine-assisted discovery and independent model assessments can prepare a
large Core inventory and highlight agreements, disagreements and missing
coverage. They do not decide whether an occurrence is semantically valid. A
human reviewer remains responsible for comparing the affected expression,
value origin, eventual consumer, tests and actual Core code before the
initiative accepts or rejects a finding.

## Rationale

The Language-All inventory produced 80 candidates from direct and inverse
searches and exposed two independent assessments through a review explorer.
In a previously reviewed `ContentObjectRenderer` case, both models described
the value as synthetic even though the initiative's code-path review had
established a persisted-record query. Agreement between models is therefore a
useful triage signal, not independent proof or authority.

## Consequences

Review tooling should preserve per-case provenance, earlier human decisions,
model-specific reasoning, evidence and direct code links. Model agreement may
prioritize or accelerate review, while every patch addition still receives a
human plausibility check. Unclear cases require deeper tracing; unrelated
calculations and other false positives must be excluded. Canonical conclusions
remain in reviewed Git-native Knowledge rather than in the explorer or model
outputs.
