## Topic 1: Mixed Mode Direction and External Demand

Eric summarized a Slack exchange initiated by Stefan Bürk, who had asked whether Mixed Mode in the Layout module would be removed, hardened or otherwise changed for TYPO3 v15. Eric had explained that no strategy for Free, Connected or Mixed Mode had been agreed for v15 because the initiative currently lacked a clear strategic counterpart in the Core Team or at Unit Lead level with whom such a direction could be aligned and decided.

Eric outlined the initiative's current structural idea: separate the structural role currently attached to the Default Language from actual language information, retain the `0` layer as an internal structural reference, and create shadow records where needed. Editors should be able to create content in the required language and position without having to understand or manage Free, Connected or Mixed Mode as technical relation models. The planned prototype for this concept remained paused while the initiative reviewed the Language-All work requested in connection with [Gerrit change 95619](https://review.typo3.org/c/Packages/TYPO3.CMS/+/95619).

During the meeting, Stefan replied that Mixed Mode had repeatedly caused problems in his previous company, where Core had been patched with guards and automatic cleanup to prohibit it. He clarified that his immediate question originated from a potential customer and concerned whether removal was planned for v15 or at all. In the subsequent Slack exchange, André stated that the initiative would like to eliminate Mixed Mode by abstracting content from structure, but that a decision required a proof of concept and was more realistic for TYPO3 v16 than v15. The exchange provided concrete external confirmation of the practical pain caused by Mixed Mode without turning the proposed structural model into an adopted Core plan.

## Topic 2: Systematic Review of Language-All Occurrences

Eric reported that a preliminary Core-wide analysis had identified 44 occurrences associated with `-1`. The intended review distinguishes values synthesized for backend selectors or other code states from values that represent a record deliberately stored with Language All in its language field. The goal is a complete finding list with an explicit semantic assessment for each occurrence, which the initiative can then validate through human review.

Eric explained that the broader analysis was still in progress and was being divided between less costly discovery work and focused semantic evaluation. The conclusions from the previous meeting had already been incorporated. The team agreed to continue the manual review of Gerrit change 95619 while the comprehensive analysis proceeded in parallel.

## Topic 3: Inline Child Language Patch Status

The WIP patch addresses the regressions previously reported by Johannes Rebhan concerning language inheritance for new inline children. The group chose not to interrupt the ongoing Language-All review for a detailed examination of this patch.

## Topic 4: LinkAnalyzer Language Semantics

The team resumed the review of Gerrit change 95619 at the `LinkAnalyzer` occurrence left open in the previous meeting. The analyzer normally carries the language value of the record containing a link, including a deliberately stored `-1`. It also falls back to `-1` when no usable record language is available, and the LinkValidator backend presents that result as All Languages.

The review therefore retained the distinction between a persisted Language-All value and a synthetic fallback even though both currently use the same integer. The occurrence was considered relevant to the marker patch, but the team did not settle whether both meanings should ultimately use the same constant or separate representations.

## Topic 5: Frontend Language Restrictions in ContentObjectRenderer

The team examined `ContentObjectRenderer::getLanguageRestriction()`, which adds language conditions to database queries for language-aware TCA tables. André and Eric reconstructed how the method behaves according to the active `LanguageAspect`, whether overlays are enabled, whether records without a Default-Language translation are included, and whether a localization-parent field is available.

For overlay-based retrieval, the initial query includes Default-Language and Language-All records before later overlay processing. Depending on the configuration, it can additionally include records in the requested language that have no Default-Language counterpart. Without overlays, the query directly selects records for the requested language together with Language-All records. The team concluded that the `-1` occurrences in this method intentionally refer to records whose language field is Language All and are therefore valid uses of the proposed marker.

## Topic 6: Category and Extbase Persistence Findings

The review continued with the frontend `CategoryCollection`. Because category relations can involve language-aware records and the implementation explicitly consults the configured language field, the team classified its Language-All comparison as another valid record-level occurrence.

The team then examined `Typo3DbQueryParser::getLanguageStatement()` in Extbase persistence. The parser builds conditions for the current language, Default-Language records and Language-All records, while later overlay handling removes results that do not match the configured mode. André also traced the subquery that resolves Default-Language record identifiers for connected overlays. The team classified the marker usages in this parser as valid references to persisted Language-All records and considered this part of the patch review complete. Eric planned to advance the comprehensive finding analysis before the next session so that later occurrences could be assessed with a clearer overall classification.
